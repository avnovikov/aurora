# Drawing doodles

Doodles are animated SVG stick figures generated from TypeScript pose specs. Never hand-draw an SVG.

## Tools

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `npm run doodles:missing [-- body,bar,…]` | Ranks the moves the planner schedules that have no doodle (seed, area, skill and joint care days, every level, three times through). Start here when choosing what to draw. |
| `npm run doodles` | Validates every spec and writes `public/doodles/doodle-<slug>.svg` and `src/data/generated/doodleMap.ts`. It does not delete the SVG of a removed spec: `git rm` it. |
| `npm run doodles:cards -- <outDir> <id>…` | One PNG per move: the Library text and 8 frames of the animation. Read every card and look at every frame. Cards draw the SVGs in `public/doodles`, so run `npm run doodles` first. `DOODLE_CARD_FRAMES=24` shows fast moments such as a turn. |

## Conventions

- `src/doodles/rig.ts` holds the types, bone lengths and solver. The registry `DOODLE_SPECS` is in `src/doodles/specs.ts`. Each batch is a `specsBatch<X>.ts`, which imports from `./rig` only, never `./specs`.
- Floor at y = 0, up is negative, and the side-view figure faces +x. Pin only the joints that define the move.
- **Depth.** In side view the far arm and far leg are drawn at half opacity behind the body, and the near limbs are drawn bright on top. Use this to show depth:
  - A bright near knee crossing the near arm reads as passing *in front of* it (outside the arm), not through it. That is how the Spiderman plank is drawn: knee to the outside of the elbow. (Not the Spiderman push-up: at push-up depth the elbow is on the torso line, so the thigh tangles with it.)
  - A pale far leg crossing behind the bright standing leg reads as passing *behind* it.
  - So crossings between limbs are fine when the near/far choice puts the right limb in front. The validator only rejects limbs through props and arms through the head.
  - Pick which side moves (near or far) so the limb that is in front in real life is the bright one.
- In front view both sides are drawn at full strength.
- **Turning round.** A side-view keyframe with `mirror: <x>` is drawn reflected about that line, so the figure faces −x. Author it facing +x as usual. Between a mirrored and an unmirrored keyframe the figure turns: it narrows to front-on and opens out facing the other way. Joint ranges are checked as authored; the mid-turn samples skip the bone-length and joint-range checks. Bench Jump uses it for "turn around and jump back": the jump back is the same keyframes mirrored about the bench. Keep the arms forward around a turn, or the turn won't show (a figure standing with arms down looks the same both ways).
- In a single view the rig keeps every bone at full length and cannot shorten a limb pointing at the viewer, so motion straight toward the camera (fire hydrant from the side, a lateral plank walk) doesn't show in one view. Draw the best single view anyway and say which plane it misses; the judge decides whether it needs a second view (see "Two views"), where foreshortening is correct.
  - A knee that bends toward the camera is the common case: in front view any lowering throws that knee 40–60 units sideways (it reads as a lateral lunge), and in side view the crossing of a Dragon Squat or curtsy lunge is sideways and invisible. These are the typical ADD VIEW cases.
  - A side-view knee that travels out to the side (Spiderman) has to swing forward under the hip, with the foot lifted, on its way to the elbow. Keep that frame brief so it doesn't read as kneeling.
- Props: bar, box, board (sloped plank: `{ kind: "board", from, to, posts? }`), floor (`{ kind: "floor" }`, sized by the renderer; opt-in), parallelBars, parallettes, plus an optional `band` between the thighs. A straight-arm support on low parallettes reads as sitting on a stool: draw it on dip bars.
- Hand-held gear (spec-level, drawn from the solved pose every frame, so it moves with the limbs; `src/doodles/gear.ts`):
  - **Rings / suspension trainer**: `straps: { anchor: [45, -460], end: "ring" }` (or `end: "handle"` for TRX handles). Both straps hang from `anchor` (the side view's hang point) to the hands. Pin hands with `onStrap(anchor, length, angle)` (angle from straight down, + towards +x) so the strap keeps its length: the validator fails a ring or handle strap whose length changes by more than 6% over the loop, or whose anchor isn't above its hand. Feet in the cradles (suspended push-up, pike): `to: "feet"` (the ankles). One strap only: `sides: ["near"]`. Keep the anchor low enough to frame (a short strap is fine); it is inside the drawing's bounds.
  - In a single `view: "front"` spec the anchor is in front-panel coordinates and used as given.
  - **Anchored band**: `straps: { anchor: [178, -12], end: "band" }` (seated band row) or `{ anchor: [-90, -60], end: "band", to: "knees" }` (terminal knee extension). Drawn in the accent colour, may stretch, anchors anywhere. Looped round the upper arm just above the elbow (single-arm band row): `{ anchor: [190, -230], end: "band", to: "elbows", sides: ["near"] }` (the band ends `ELBOW_LOOP` = 0.8 of the way from shoulder to elbow).
  - **Band between the hands**: `handBand: true` (pull-apart). In the side view the hands overlap, so draw it in two views.
  - **Kettlebell**: `kettlebell: { hands: ["near"], size?: 14 }`: a solid bell with a handle arc up to the grip (never a second head). Two hands on one bell (two-hand swing, goblet): list one hand and put both hands together. Per keyframe `bell` says where it sits, blended over the transitions: `"hang"` (default: straight below the grip; rows, deadlifts, goblet by the chest), `"swing"` (on along the forearm, flung out: swings, snatch pulls), `"rack"` (on the back of the forearm by the wrist: front rack and overhead; behind the forearm in the side view, so author racked frames facing +x; a `rack` (either hand) on a spec with any `mirror` keyframe is a validator error). A bell set down on the floor: hands at y = `kettlebellOnFloor(size)`; the validator fails a bell that goes through the floor.
    - **Per hand**: `bell: { near: "rack", far: "hang" }` sets each hand's bell (a hand left out hangs): alternating hang clean, double-bell windmill (one overhead, one on the floor). The string form still sets every bell.
    - **Changing hands** (figure 8, pass between the legs, lunge pass-through): one bell (`kettlebell: { hands: ["near"] }`, the hand it starts in) and `bellIn: "far"` on the keyframe where the far hand takes it; it stays there until a keyframe says `bellIn` again (round the loop). The keyframe where the holder changes is the hand-off: put both hands together on the bell there; the bell rides in the old hand up to it and in the new hand after. The validator fails a hand-off whose hand tips are more than 1.5 bell radii apart (in 3D in two views, so give the hands the same `sideways` z). Example: `frames: [{ pose: front, bellIn: "near", … }, { pose: handsTogetherBetweenTheLegs, bellIn: "far", sideways: { hands: { near: 0, far: 0 } }, … }, …]`. A hand-off bell is drawn at full strength over the figure, unless the spec sets `fadeBehind` (see "Two views"), which draws it pale behind the body while it passes behind the back.
    - **By the horns** (halo, overhead triceps extension, goblet held by the horns): `bell: "horns"` with one bell in `kettlebell.hands`: the bell sits at the hands' midpoint, its body on beyond the joined hands along the mean forearm (elbow to hand tip), foreshortened with the forearms. Both hands must be on it (tips within 1.5 bell radii, like a hand-off). In two views it is drawn twice, once behind the torso and once over the figure, shown only while the bell is nearer the viewer than the head, so a halo's bell disappears behind the head in the front panel and passes behind it (at head height) in the side panel. One view draws it over the figure: draw halos in two views. Example: `{ pose: { …, hands: { near: [62, -250], far: [62, -250] } }, bell: "horns", sideways: { hands: { near: 0, far: 0 } } }`, then the hands round the head at about shoulder-to-chin height with the elbows out (`elbows: { near: { out: 90 }, … }`).
  - **Stick** (shoulder dislocates, round-the-world, stick good mornings): `stick: {}` or `stick: { overhang: 30 }`. Drawn in ink through both hand tips, `overhang` (default `STICK_OVERHANG` = 24) past each hand. It points at the side camera when the hands are wide, so the side view sees it end-on (a dot in the hands): draw dislocates in **two views** with the grip in `sideways`, e.g. `frames: [{ pose: { …, hands: { near: hand, far: hand } }, sideways: { hands: { near: -70, far: 70 } } }, …]` with the hands on circles round the shoulder at the 3D reach (`Math.sqrt(125.5 ** 2 - (70 - 22) ** 2)` from `[chest x, chest y - 18]`). Between keyframes each hand keeps its sideways offset (planes of constant z), so 90° keyframe steps keep the grip; the validator fails a grip width that changes by more than 5% (`STICK_GIVE`), hands closer than 20 in two views, and a stick through the head. In the front panel it is drawn behind the torso and head while the hands are behind the body, over the figure while they are in front. Top view: drawn from above.
  - **Towel / strap** in both hands: `towel: {}` taut from hand to hand (towel triceps extension, towel pull-apart), or `towel: { around: "near-foot" }` (or `"far-foot"`) looped round that foot's sole (`TOWEL_WRAP` = 0.55 of the way from ankle to toe): hands → sole → hands (lying towel hamstring stretch). Drawn in the accent colour like a band. Pin the hands so the towel keeps its length: aim the hands at the sole from the shoulders and stop the towel's length short of it; the validator fails a length that changes by more than 12% (`TOWEL_SLACK`) and a towel through the head. Example: `towel: { around: "near-foot" }, frames: [{ pose: { ...supineTorso([0, -22], 79.6), hands: { near: hand, far: hand }, feet: { near: along([0, -22], -72, 173.4), far: [173.2, -8] } } }, …]` with `hand = along(shoulder, angle(shoulder, sole), dist(shoulder, sole) - 112)`.
  - One of `stick`, `towel` and `handBand` per spec.

## Head facing

The head is a circle, so a keyframe says which way it faces with `face: { turn?, tilt?, nod? }` (degrees, relative to the torso; blended over the transitions). A short nose tick then shows the facing on every panel, foreshortened in 3D: a profile nose in the side view, a dot in the middle of the face when it looks at the viewer, a tick across the face as it turns. Leave `face` out and no tick is drawn.

- `turn` (neck rotation, up to 90): + towards the far side (+z, the figure's left), - towards the near side (the side camera). The front panel shows it (the tick slides to the viewer's left for -turn); the side view sees the tick shorten onto the face (towards the camera) or disappear behind the head (away). Draw head turns in two views.
- `nod` (up to 60): + chin to the chest, - looking up (at the ceiling). It turns the head forward or back about the neck joint, so the side view shows it. With `pose.head` pinned the head stays where pinned and only the face turns: a **chin tuck** pins the head about 12 back (`head: [-12, -306]` on a standing figure with the chest at `[0, -252]`) and keeps the face level (`face: { nod: 6 }`).
- `tilt` (ear to shoulder, up to 45): + towards the far shoulder. It swings the head sideways on the neck, so it needs a front (or top) panel: in a side-only spec the validator rejects it.
- Example (look left and right, two views): `views: ["side", "front"], frames: [{ pose: stand, hold: 0.4, face: { turn: 0 } }, { pose: stand, hold: 0.6, face: { turn: -75 } }, { pose: stand, hold: 0.4, face: { turn: 0 } }, { pose: stand, hold: 0.6, face: { turn: 75 } }]`. Neck circles: a ring of keyframes through nod +/- and tilt +/-.
- Not with `mirror` (turning round). The neck's bend check (60°) still applies.

## Partner figure

Partner-assisted stretches draw a second figure, paler (`PARTNER_OPACITY`) and thinner, behind the exerciser, on the same clock: `partner: { frames, mirror?, holds? }` with **one partner keyframe per exerciser keyframe** (`{ pose, sideways?, face? }`, no holds: it uses the exerciser's holds and `transition`).

- Author the partner facing +x like any pose; `mirror: <x>` draws it reflected about that line so it faces the exerciser (a partner at the feet of someone lying face up). Pin its pelvis.
- `holds: [{ hand: "near", on: "ankle.near" }, { hand: "far", on: "knee.near", offset?: [dx, dy] }]` keep the partner's hands on the exerciser's joints on **every** sample: they are pinned at each keyframe (reflected into the partner's frame for you) and the arm is re-solved onto the joint in between. `on` is a joint as drawn (`pelvis`, `chest`, `head`, `shoulder|elbow|wrist|hand|hip|knee|ankle|toe.<side>`) or the middle of a bone (`upperArm|forearm|thigh|shin.<side>`). Don't also pin a held hand in the pose. Place the partner within arm's reach (126) of every held joint: the validator reports a hold that slips off (out of reach) and the partner's own anatomy, pins and floor, prefixed `partner:`.
- Example (partner-assisted lying hamstring stretch): `frames: [{ pose: lying(-72), hold: 0.5 }, { pose: lying(-100), hold: 1 }], partner: { mirror: 60, holds: [{ hand: "near", on: "ankle.near" }, { hand: "far", on: "knee.near" }], frames: [{ pose: kneel(5) }, { pose: kneel(28) }] }` where `lying(deg)` raises the near leg at `deg` and `kneel(lean)` is a tall kneel (pelvis `[0, -92]`, knees at `[±2, -4]`, shins back) leaning the chest `lean`° forward to push.
- One view is the default (the side view reads a partner best). Two views work too (`sideways` per partner keyframe; it is drawn behind the exerciser on both panels).
- Not with `mirror` keyframes on the exerciser. The partner carries no gear.

## Two views (only when the judge asks)

Every move is drawn in **one view first**. A second view is added only when the independent reviewer gives it an **ADD VIEW** verdict, because one view hides or misleads about a plane that matters (archer push-up: the straight arm slides out to the side). A plain push-up stays one view.

- Opt in with `views: ["side", "front"]` on the spec. The figure is solved in 3D and drawn twice, side panel left and front panel right, same scale, one clock. The front panel looks at the figure's face: near side (z < 0) on the viewer's left, far side on the right.
- **Top view for floor moves**: `views: ["side", "top"]` (`TOP_VIEWS` in `sideways.ts`) draws the side panel with a view from **above** under it. Use it when the move happens on the floor and the second plane is sideways: from the front a plank, a supine or a seated figure collapses behind its head, from above it lies flat. Plank hip dips (hips swinging to each side under still shoulders, hands and feet), floor windshield wipers (straight legs sweeping side to side), 90/90 hip switches (bent knees swinging from one side to the other), fire hydrants, clamshells.
  - Layout: the top panel sits **under** the side panel with the **same x**, so the head, hands and feet line up vertically between the panels. The top panel's vertical is z: the **near side (z < 0, the side view's camera) at the bottom**, the far side at the top. Labels "side" and "top" under each panel.
  - Both sides are drawn at full strength; draw order is height, so the higher part is drawn over the lower one (the head over the torso of a seated figure, raised legs over the floor).
  - Props from above: box, bench board and bar as their footprint (x extent × front extent: box ±`frontHalf`, board ±36, bar ±90), parallel-bar rails and parallette handles as lines along x at their z. The floor is the panel itself and isn't drawn.
  - Authoring is the same `sideways` as for the front view, with the same 3D checks. Example (windshield wiper, legs swept to the near side): `pose: { pelvis: [0, -20], chest: [-80, -24], head: [-136, -28], hands: { near: [-62, -4], far: [-62, -4] }, feet: { near: { dir: -90, length: 172 }, far: { dir: -90, length: 172 } } }, sideways: { hands: { near: -140, far: 140 }, feet: { near: -120, far: -104 } }`.
  - Not yet from above: straps, kettlebells, a band between the hands, the ab wheel (validator error). Choose one second view: front or top, not both.
- Author facing +x in side view as usual, then give a keyframe `sideways` positions (absolute z): `sideways: { hands: { far: 90 }, feet: { near: -40 }, elbows: { near: { out: 70 } }, knees: { far: { at: 60 } }, pelvis: 0 }`. Anything not given keeps its natural width: shoulders ±22, hips ±8, spine at `pelvis` (default 0).
- Give a knee or foot that goes sideways as a **direction** instead of a point: `knees: { near: { dir: 40 } }` (thigh angle from the hip), `feet: { near: { dir: 170 } }` (shin from that knee, or a straight leg from the hip when the knee isn't pinned; `length` overrides the bone length). The solver shortens it in the side view by the joint's z from the keyframe's `sideways`, so don't retype z offsets. Planted straight legs with feet out to the side: `spreadFeet: true` on the pose. `lockedY3` (in `sideways.ts`) still finds the shoulder height at which wide arms lock. A hinted knee or foot follows its own keyframe's `sideways`, so a pose function reused on keyframes with different `sideways` foreshortens differently on each.
- `out` (degrees) rolls a knee's or elbow's bend outward from the forward plane: 0 is today's bend, 90 points the joint straight out to the side (flared elbows, a fire-hydrant knee).
- Each bone keeps its true length in 3D, so a limb that goes sideways is drawn **shorter in the side view**. That's correct, not a bug.
- **Pin the chest** when hands go wide. Without it the 2D spine leans as if the hands were straight ahead and the arm can't reach (`hands.far unreachable`).
- Gear in two views: `straps`, `handBand` and `kettlebell` draw on both panels (`wheel` doesn't yet). Front panel: each strap hangs from a fixed point at the anchor's height: `straps.width` either side of the centre line (near at z = -width, far at +width; `width: 0` is one shared anchor) or, without `width`, straight above where its hand (knee, ankle) is in the first keyframe. When the hands travel wide (fly, face pull, Y raise), set `width` to where the straps really hang (e.g. the open hands' z, so the straps hang plumb at the top and slant in when the hands close) and pin the side-view hands so each strap keeps its 3D length to that anchor: the validator measures the strap from (anchor x, anchor y, ±width). Example: `straps: { anchor: FLY_A, end: "handle", width: 90 }` with the hand from circles of the arm round the shoulder (z ±22) and of the strap round the anchor (z ±90); a racked bell sits outside its forearm (the hand's own side); a band joins the hands across. Front draw order follows depth: gear goes with its limb. Example: `views: ["side", "front"], straps: { anchor: [-230, -400], end: "handle", to: "feet" }` with `sideways: { hands: { near: -40, far: 40 }, feet: { near: -10, far: 10 } }` on every keyframe.
- **Sideways torso lean and hip shift**: `sideways.chest` is the z of the chest (the shoulders' centre); it defaults to the pelvis z, so `pelvis` alone still shifts the whole spine. When they differ the spine leans: it turns rigidly (true bone lengths) until pelvis and chest sit at their z, so it tilts in the front view and draws **shorter in the side view**. `sideways.pivot` says which end keeps its side-view (x, y); give the same pivot on every leaning keyframe of the move (the validator fails differing pivots: the lean blends about the first keyframe's pivot, so a change makes the spine jump). A lean wider than the spine is long before it leans (the 2D pelvis to chest, 80 when straight) is reported rather than silently clamped.
  - `pivot: "pelvis"` (default): the whole spine, shoulders and head turn about the pelvis; the hips stay level, the shoulders tilt, the chest comes down and in. Side bends, windmills, bent press: `{ pose: { pelvis: [0, -164], chest: [0, -244], … }, sideways: { chest: -48, feet: { near: -40, far: 40 } } }` leans towards the near side. Work out hand pins from the leaned shoulders (`solveKeyframe3(solvePose(pose), pose, sideways).arms.far.root`), e.g. a top arm straight up over its shoulder.
  - `pivot: "chest"`: the chest, shoulders, neck and head stay put and square (arm pins stay valid); only the lower spine swings, and the hips turn with it. Plank hip dips: `sideways: { pelvis: ±34, chest: 0, pivot: "chest", feet: { near: -10, far: 10 } }` with the 2D pelvis a little lower. The side view sees the pelvis closer to the chest, so pin straight legs a little short (or search for the straightest legs that still reach the feet).
  - Pin the feet's z when the pelvis moves: a foot without its own z follows its hip.
  - The 3D validator checks spine bends in 3D, so a chest pivot that swings the hips far (more than about 35° of lean) is reported as a thoracic bend.
- Props in two views: `bar`, `box`, `board`, `floor`, `parallelBars` and `parallettes`. A box is 120 wide from the front; `frontHalf` narrows it (a seat narrower than spread knees, so the shins don't run along its sides). A board is seen end-on as its outline with two legs; each panel sizes its own floor. No `mirror` (turning round) and no `view: "front"` together with `views`.
  - **Parallel bars** are seen end-on: each rail a small square on an upright, at ±`frontHalf` (default 30). Put the hands on the rails: `props: [{ kind: "parallelBars", y: -216, x1: -190, x2: 190 }]` with `sideways: { hands: { near: -30, far: 30 } }` on every keyframe (dips, archer dips: the forward lean stays in the side panel, the arms' width in the front panel).
  - **Parallettes** are seen end-on at each hand's z in the **first** keyframe (a handle on an upright with a foot on the floor), like straps: `props: [{ kind: "parallettes", y: -40, xs: [60] }]` with `sideways: { hands: { near: -34, far: 34 } }`.
- **Props have depth in two views.** A limb that crosses a prop in the side view only counts as crossing it when, at the crossing, its z is inside the prop's front extent: box ±`frontHalf` (60), board ±36, bar ±90, each parallel-bar rail ±4 about ±`frontHalf`, each parallette ±12 about its hand. So a leg hanging off the side of a bench passes beside it: lie the figure near the edge (`sideways: { pelvis: -22 }`), narrow the bench (`frontHalf: 30`) and give the hanging knee and foot their z (`knees: { near: { at: -46 } }, feet: { near: -52 }`). The thigh leaves the hip inside the bench's width, so keep it above the top until it is out past the edge. The head is checked the same way. One-view doodles keep the flat check: every prop is solid in the drawing plane.
- **Spine roll (twists)**: `sideways.twist` (degrees) turns the shoulder line about the upper spine; + brings the **far** shoulder forward (towards the chest's front: +x when upright) and the near one back. `sideways.hipTwist` does the same for the hip line about the lower spine. Both blend linearly over transitions (90 → 170 rolls through 130; split a sweep past ~150° with a middle keyframe so the arms don't swing the short way through the body). The arms hang from, and are pinned from, the turned shoulders, so work hand pins out from them: a shoulder sits at the shoulder centre (chest + 18 up the spine) plus 22 along `s = cos(twist)·side + sin(twist)·front`. In the side view a turned chest splits the shoulders fore and aft, in the front view they narrow, from above the shoulder line turns. A `face` is relative to the chest, so the head turns with it; to keep looking ahead set `face: { turn: twist }`. Limits: each roll within ±180, the spine's own twist (`twist - hipTwist`) within 90.
  - Standing torso rotation, arms out (side + top): `sideways: { twist: 60, hands: { near: -73.5, far: 73.5 } }` with `hands: { near: [-127.3, -270], far: [127.3, -270] }` (147 out along the turned shoulder line from the shoulder centre `[0, -270]`). Russian twist, lunge with a twist, half-kneeling thoracic rotation: the same with the hands where the turned arms put them.
  - **Side-lying** (open book, side-lying thoracic rotation, Bretzel): lay the 2D spine flat (`pelvis: [0, -24], chest: [-80, -24]`, head to -x, which is face up at roll 0) and roll the whole body 90: `twist: -90, hipTwist: -90` lies on the far side with the back to the camera, shoulders and hips stacked (near on top, 22 and 8 above the spine). The chest opens by rolling the shoulders back towards 0 (`twist: -10`, hips staying at -90), the top (near, bright) arm sweeping up over the shoulder to the floor behind. Knees bent forward: pin them with points and `sideways.knees.<side>.at` at the thigh's length from the stacked hips (direction hints ignore `hipTwist`), and feet pointed (`toes: 0`). Side + top shows it best (the shoulder line opening from a point into a line, the arm crossing from one side to the other); side + front looks along the spine and sees the arm's arc, with the torso behind the head circle.
- **Shoulder girdle** (shrugs, shoulder circles, protraction / retraction): `sideways.shoulders: { near?: { up?, forward? }, far?: { … } }` moves each shoulder relative to the chest, in the chest's own frame (up the spine, towards the chest's front, so `forward` follows a twist). The collar line (chest to shoulder) and the whole arm move with it; blended over transitions. Limits: `up` ±14 (`GIRDLE_LIMITS`), `forward` ±12. Shrug (side + front): `frames: [{ pose: stand, hold: 0.4, sideways: { shoulders: { near: { up: -6 }, far: { up: -6 } } } }, { pose: stand, hold: 0.6, sideways: { shoulders: { near: { up: 14 }, far: { up: 14 } } } }]`. Shoulder circles: eight keyframes round `up = 14 cos a, forward = 12 sin a` with `hold: 0`, `transition: 0.25`; the side view shows the shoulder (and the hanging arm) tracing the loop, the front view the rise and fall. Small: the front view reads a shrug clearly, the side view only by the arm rising; leave the hands unpinned so they ride with the shoulder.
- **Sideways foot and hand angles** (ankle inversion / eversion, wrist radial / ulnar deviation): `sideways.toes: { near?, far? }` and `sideways.wrists: { near?, far? }` (degrees, up to `END_TURN_LIMIT` = 60) turn the foot or hand out of the side view's plane, + towards +z (the far side: the near foot's toe turns in, the far foot's out), keeping its side-view direction (`pose.toes`, or square to the shin; `pose.wrists`, which a wrist angle needs, since a straight hand tip is pinned). Blended over the transitions; the side view sees the foot or hand shorter. Ankle circles: `toes: { near: 24 + 46 cos φ }` with `sideways: { toes: { near: 40 sin φ } }` over eight keyframes round φ, so the toe dips and rises in the side view and swings in and out in the front view. Wrist circles: `wrists` and `sideways.wrists` the same way, forearms pointing straight ahead so the front view looks down them and each hand tip circles its wrist.
- **Zoom inset in two views**: a two-view doodle gets its `focus` inset only when the focus names the panel to zoom on, `panel: "side" | "front" | "top"` (its `joints` as drawn on that panel); the inset sits right of both panels. Small joints (ankle and wrist circles) need it: `hf_167: { joints: ["wrist.near", "hand.near"], …, zoom: 8, margin: 6, panel: "front" }`. A joint seen end-on (a forearm pointing at the viewer) leaves only the hand stub to read: angle the upper arm or shin a little so the stub never runs along it.
- **Behind the body (`fadeBehind: true`)**: in the front panel both arms are drawn bright, so a forearm reaching behind the back or behind the head reads as in front of it. With `fadeBehind` on the spec, a forearm whose middle is behind the torso or head (along the panel's depth, within the body's width; nothing is behind above the top of the head) is drawn pale just behind the torso instead, and a hand-off bell held by such a forearm goes pale with it (a horns bell: while it is behind the head). In the side panel the hand-off bell goes pale while it is on the far side of the body. Opt-in so existing doodles stay as drawn. Used by the single-leg balance with bell circles (bell passed round the back) and the arm and neck stretch (the folded forearm behind the head).
- Twists are not an automatic leave-out: show a twist through what moves sideways or across — an elbow travelling to the opposite knee, hands passing from one side of the hips to the other, legs swinging side to side (windshield wiper), hips dipping to each side — with depth shading and, where one view can't show it, two views.
